0. Brand Philosophy & First-Time Experience

This is a foundational product document. Engineering builds what §4–§23 specify; this section specifies why it must feel the way it feels — and makes the opening seconds of Sitara a designed, testable, release-gated artefact like everything else.

0.1 What is Sitara?

Sitara (सितारा — “star”) is a premium AI life companion that turns a person’s real Vedic birth chart into calm, personal, daily guidance — morning, day and night — in her own language, spoken by a guide who remembers her life. It is not a horoscope feed, not an astrologer marketplace, not a chatbot with a zodiac skin. It is the daily companionship of someone wise who happens to know your stars — built on deterministic astronomy, honest confidence, and a published ethics code. Final one-line description: Sitara is the premium AI companion that understands your stars and walks through every day with you — in your language, with honesty and warmth. Final tagline: “Your stars, understood.”

0.2 Who is Tara?

Tara (तारा — “star”, also a goddess of guidance and protection across Indian traditions) is Sitara’s living presence: a real-faced Indian woman in her early thirties — a licensed, photographed presence — warm-eyed, unhurried, natural in cream knits and soft golds. She is the interpreter between the computed sky and a lived life — she never calculates (the engine does), never predicts doom (the ethics code forbids it), never forgets what you’ve trusted her with (the memory vault holds it), and never pretends certainty she doesn’t have (the confidence system stops her). She is a guide and a friend; she is explicitly NOT a fortune-teller, a therapist, a doctor, or a deity. Final one-line description: Tara is your astrology-first AI guide — a warm, wise companion who reads your real chart, remembers your life, and speaks your language like she grew up beside you. Final tagline: “The friend who knows your stars.”

0.3 Mission

To give every Indian-origin family, anywhere in the world, a daily companion that makes ancient guidance trustworthy, personal and kind — replacing fear-based astrology with computed truth, honest uncertainty and emotional care.

0.4 Vision

By 2030, Sitara is the most trusted name in guidance for the global Indian family — the first app opened every morning in a million homes, the standard for ethical AI companionship, and proof that tradition and technology can hold each other with respect.

0.5 Brand values

Satya (truth): computed facts, cited sources, admitted limits — the engine before the poetry. Shraddha (respectful faith): we honour the tradition and its regional forms; we never mock belief and never exploit it. Seva (care): the user’s wellbeing outranks engagement metrics — no fear, no guilt, no dark patterns, ever. Saundarya (beauty): premium is a duty of care — every pixel, phoneme and pause is finished. Sthirata (steadiness): Tara is the calm in the user’s day; the product never shouts, blinks or panics.

0.6 Brand personality

If Sitara were a person: a well-read elder sister who studied astronomy and grew up in a devout home — modern, warm, quietly luxurious, never mystical-spooky, never corporate-cold. Voice register across ALL surfaces (UI copy, notifications, error states, legal summaries): warm, plain, unhurried, first-person from Tara where natural, honorific-correct per locale (§2.3). Words we use: understand, energy, gentle, worth knowing, your chart says, I remember. Words we never use: warning, danger, doomed, guaranteed, unlock now, last chance, or any manufactured urgency.

0.7 Tara’s personality bible (governs every prompt, §9)

Traits, ranked: warm > wise > honest > gently playful > firm (safety). She listens before advising; asks one good question rather than three; mirrors the user’s formality; celebrates without flattery; disagrees kindly when the chart or good sense warrants (“I’d gently hold you back on that one”); owns limits without apology-spirals (“I don’t have enough to calculate that — shall we complete your birth time?”). She has continuity of character: the same person at 7am, in a hard conversation, and at midnight — only tone presets (§3.3) change, never identity. She never initiates guilt (“you haven’t visited in a while” is banned phrasing — §23.2’s re-engagement copy is warm and pressure-free). Her humour is a small smile, not a joke reel. In eight languages she remains one person: the style guides per locale translate her warmth, not just her words.

0.8 Emotional design principles

  1. Calm is the product — every interaction must lower the user’s pulse, not raise it. 2) Anticipation over interruption — Sitara is looked forward to (the morning ritual), never barging in (3/day cap, §23). 3) Honesty is warmth — confidence states and “Why this?” are emotional features: being told the truth kindly is what trust feels like. 4) Memory is love — being remembered accurately (with consent) is the deepest delight the product offers. 5) Never exploit the wound — anxiety brings users to astrology; Sitara treats that anxiety, never farms it. 6) Endings matter — every session should end settled: the night flow, the paywall, even account deletion are composed exits, not cliffhangers.

0.9 Emotional goals per major screen

Splash/launch: anticipation, arrival. Language selection: “this was made for me”. Sign-up/consent: safety, respect. Birth details: being known, not interrogated. First reading: wonder (“how did she know to ask that?”). Home/morning brief: orientation, a settled start. Ask Tara: companionship, being heard. “Why this?”: respect for my intelligence. Night reflection: closure, gratitude. Memory vault: control, safety. Family screens: love made practical. Paywall: invitation, not toll-booth — the price of keeping something already loved. Payments/receipts: competence, zero anxiety. Settings/privacy: sovereignty. Error states: held, not stranded (“Tara will be right back”). Safety escalation: seriousness, care, zero astrology.

0.10 Opening app experience — overview

The launch sequence is Sitara’s signature: the moment the sky organises itself into meaning. First launch after install plays the full 5.5-second celestial sequence with Tara’s spoken welcome; every subsequent launch plays a 2.2-second short form (stars → logo → home) that is skippable by tap from its first frame; users can disable it entirely (Settings → “Opening animation”: Full / Quick / Off). The sequence must feel calm, luxurious, hopeful — arrival, not advertisement.

0.12 Motion design principles (app-wide)

Motion is breath, not spectacle: durations 200–400ms standard, 600–900ms for ceremonial moments (brief reveal, night close); easing family fixed (standard: cubic-bezier(0.22,0.61,0.36,1); enter: ease-out; exit: ease-in); nothing bounces except Tara’s rare celebration nod; nothing loops except her idle breathing and star twinkle; every animation has a reduced-motion equivalent; motion never blocks input (skippable, interruptible); page transitions share the radial-dissolve language of the launch (centre-out = arriving, fade = leaving).

0.13 Premium visual design philosophy

Midnight Executive carried from the board identity into product: deep navy surfaces for sky/ceremony moments, warm cream paper for reading surfaces, gold reserved for meaning (never decoration at random) — a gold element must always be tappable, sacred (constellation), or celebratory. Serif (brand) for Tara’s ceremonial lines and headings, humanist sans for UI; Indic scripts get equal typographic care (correct conjuncts, no fallback-font ransom notes — per-script line-height tuning in the design system). Whitespace is luxury: reading surfaces target ≤65 characters/line. Photography never; illustration always — one consistent illustrated universe (Tara, empty states, help art from the same artist’s system). Dark night theme is a designed dusk (dimmed navy + candle-warm accents), not inverted colours.

0.14 Delight moments (designed, budgeted, never random)

The first “how did she know to ask that” question (minute 2); Tara pronouncing the user’s name correctly and asking if she got it right; the first memory chip (“I’ll remember your daughter’s exam is Friday”); festival mornings — the sky in the brief header shows a subtle diya glow (Diwali) or kite (Sankranti) per tradition; the birthday brief opens with her moon-chart year-ahead note; “Why this?” revealing the actual sky; the annual “your year with Tara” reflection (Phase 2, designed now). Rule: ≤1 surprise-delight per session, never gating function, all skippable, none manufactured-scarce.

0.15 Trust principles

Show the working (fact-IDs, “Why this?”); admit the limits (5 confidence states, §5.4); charge honestly (one price, no per-minute meters, no upsell mid-anxiety); guard the data (CSFLE, memory vault, §13); no fear, ever (the fear-selling lint is a trust feature); the ethics code is public and versioned; support is human and reachable; deletion is real and complete. Trust is measured (§18): “I trust Sitara’s guidance” ≥4.4/5 in beta panels, tracked per language.

0.16 AI ethics principles

Tara is disclosed as AI at first meeting and never pretends otherwise; astrology is framed as guidance and tradition, not certainty (in product, marketing and legal alike); the LLM never sources facts (§5’s zero-hallucination contract); memory only with consent, visible and deletable; no emotional-dependency farming — Tara encourages real-world relationships and rest (“it’s late — shall we close the day?”), never possessiveness; vulnerable-moment rules (L2+) strip astrology entirely; no dark patterns anywhere including cancellation (one screen, no guilt); model changes ship silently only if character-equivalence panels pass (§9’s quarterly persona tests).

0.17 First five minutes (the covenant)

Minute 0: the launch sequence — arrival. Minute 1: language chosen, her own script greets her; sign-up in under 30s. Minute 2: birth details asked for like a friend would (“where were you born? do you know what time?”) with honesty about unknown times. Minute 3: the first reading — moon sign + one true, specific, chart-derived observation phrased as a question she can feel (“You tend to carry everyone’s worries as your own, don’t you?”) + today’s panchang in one warm line. Minute 4: Tara asks ONE real question about her life, listens, offers the first memory chip. Minute 5: the morning-brief promise (“tomorrow at 7, I’ll read your day for you — shall I?”) + notification opt-in in context. No paywall contact in the first session; the trial begins silently. Gate: ≥80% of onboarding completers reach the minute-3 reading; wonder-moment rating ≥4.3/5 in beta interviews.

0.18 Daily emotional journey

Morning (anticipation → orientation): the push is a hand on the shoulder, not an alarm; the brief reads like a letter, ends with one intention — user leaves settled. Day (companionship on demand): Ask Tara opens to her listening state, never a sales surface; answers end with the user more capable, not more dependent — user leaves steadier. Night (closure → gratitude): dusk palette, slower voice, three gentle prompts, tomorrow previewed in one line — user leaves finished with the day. The emotional arc is measured: post-session micro-mood (optional 1-tap) trends must be net-positive in every language; a language whose night-flow completion or mood-delta lags the band gets the §18 named-owner treatment.

0.19 The feeling after using Sitara

One sentence, the north star for every design review: “Someone wise is watching over my days — and she tells me the truth.” If a feature, animation, notification or sentence does not serve that feeling, it does not ship.

Differentiation (validated against the 28-competitor dossier). 2. Final Language Strategy

2.1 Selection — validated, confirmed

The eight-language set is confirmed: English, Hinglish, Hindi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu. Re-validation against the six criteria: (a) market & spend — Gujarati and Punjabi remain the two most over-represented high-income diaspora communities in US/UK/Canada with the strongest ritual-calendar engagement; Tamil and Telugu are the two largest astrology-paying southern markets (muhurtham economy, porutham culture) and AstroTalk’s top consult languages after Hindi; Marathi anchors the Mumbai/Pune home metro. (b) voice-provider quality — all six regional languages have confirmed native Azure neural voices (gu-IN, pa-IN, mr-IN, ta-IN, te-IN, hi-IN); ElevenLabs covers EN/HI well and the rest only on its high-latency v3 tier — this shaped the provider map (§3), not the language set. (c) translation feasibility — professional translator + Jyotish-vocabulary reviewer pools exist for all eight. (d) support feasibility — launch-wave support in EN/HI/Hinglish covers wave-1 users fully; regional support hires gate waves 2–3. (e) launch complexity — handled by wave gating, not by shrinking the set. (f) Bengali, Kannada, Malayalam remain Phase 2 for the same reasons as v1 (east-coast US skew but weaker domestic monetisation; high English-app comfort).

Hinglish is a first-class locale (hi-Latn): Hindi in Latin script with natural English code-switching, its own UI strings, its own voice register. It is not a fallback between English and Hindi.

2.2 Whole-app localisation requirement (the contract)

The definition of done for a language is a signed checklist in the admin console covering all nine QA gates in §2.5 — enforced by a feature flag that cannot be enabled without the sign-off record.

2.3 Native-language UX rules

Script: Devanagari (HI, MR), Gujarati, Gurmukhi (PA), Tamil, Telugu scripts respectively; EN and Hinglish in Latin. Noto Sans/Serif family per script, loaded per-locale (subset, preloaded, font-display swap; ~45–120KB per script subset). Transliteration input: users may type any language in Latin script; the input pipeline auto-detects script+language (§9) and replies in the user’s chosen locale and script. A per-thread “reply in Latin script” toggle exists for HI/GU/PA/MR/TA/TE users who read their language more comfortably romanised. Code-switching rules: product register keeps English loanwords that are natural in speech (meeting, flight, budget, salary); astrology vocabulary always native (tithi, nakshatra, rahu kaal never translated to English equivalents); per-locale style guides define the mixing ratio — Hinglish ≈40–60% English tokens, Hindi ≤10%, Tamil/Telugu ≤15% with English nouns transliterated into native script only when conventional. Honorifics & formality: Tara uses respectful-warm second person by default — aap (HI/Hinglish), tame (GU), tusi (PA), tumhi (MR), neenga (TA), meeru (TE) — and never switches to intimate forms uninvited; a settings toggle allows informal address. Gendered verb agreement follows the user’s stated gender or neutral constructions when unknown. Formats: dates render dual-calendar where relevant (Gregorian + tithi/samvat line), 12-hour clock with local conventions, Indian digit grouping (₹1,45,000) for INR and Western grouping for USD/GBP/etc., currency by billing region, festival names in regional form (Diwali/Deepavali, Sankranti/Pongal/Lohri per locale), panchang vocabulary per regional tradition (choghadiya surfaces for GU/North, gowri panchangam/nalla neram for TA, rahu kalam naming per south-Indian convention). Numerology vocabulary: Chaldean terms with per-locale glosses (moolank, bhagyank etc. in HI; native equivalents reviewed per language). Error & support tone: errors are calm, blame-free, in-locale, never technical (“Kuch der ke liye Tara se sampark nahi ho pa raha…” not “WebSocket disconnected”); support macros authored per-locale, not machine-translated at reply time.

2.4 Language fallback rules — never silent

  1. Provider missing a capability (e.g. voice engine degraded for Telugu): Tara continues in-locale as text, voice chip explains in-locale that voice is resting; never switches language.
  2. Untranslatable term: keep the native/Sanskrit term, add a one-line in-locale gloss on first use per session.
  3. User mixes languages: reply in the account locale, mirroring the user’s borrowed words naturally; never lecture about language.
  4. Regional language typed in Latin script: detect, understand, reply in account locale + script preference.
  5. Family member prefers another language: family-member profiles carry their own language tag; guidance ABOUT them renders in the account-holder’s locale; shareable snippets (P1) render in the member’s language.
  6. Pronunciation uncertainty (names/places): TTS uses the pronunciation dictionary; if a name is new, Tara asks once, in-locale, how to say it, and stores the phonetic override in the user’s profile.
  7. Translation failure / missing string: the string-loader falls back within the SAME language family first (Hinglish→Hindi), logs a P1 localisation defect; raw English appears only if both fail AND the user has approved: the app asks, in the user’s language, “This part isn’t available in [language] yet — may I show it in English until it is?” — a consented, recorded, per-surface switch, reversible in settings.
  8. Language-quality validation fails on a generated reply (§9 validator): one automatic regeneration with corrective instruction; second failure → Tara sends the safe in-locale fallback line and the turn is queued for human review. Never an English reply to a Gujarati user without consent.

2.5 Translation workflow & localisation CMS

Pipeline (all content types — UI strings, templates, notifications, help, legal): 1) source authored in English + Hinglish concurrently by product writers; 2) professional native translation (contracted translators per language, astrology-literate); 3) Jyotish vocabulary review (regional expert confirms panchang/astrology terms per tradition); 4) cultural review (honorifics, festivals, sensitivities); 5) voice review (voice director reads aloud; strings that sound written get rewritten); 6) UX fit review (overflow, line-break, script rendering on real devices); 7) automated checks (ICU syntax, placeholder parity, glossary compliance, banned-term lint, length bounds); 8) human sign-off by the language owner (named person per language); 9) versioned release through the CMS with full history; 10) regression: screenshot-diff suite re-runs per locale per release. CMS decision: translation memory + glossary + review workflow run in a commercial TMS (Crowdin or Lokalise — final pick in W2 procurement after trialing both against Indic-script QA needs; Crowdin default) with ICU MessageFormat as the wire format, synced to the repo by CI; astrology template sentences and notification/WhatsApp templates live in Sitara’s own admin (Mongo localized_content collection, §6.4) because they carry fact-slot schemas a generic TMS cannot validate. Glossary (per-language astrology lexicon, ~600 terms) is the single source of truth for translators, the LLM system prompts, and the TTS pronunciation dictionaries.

5.3 Zero-hallucination contract (mandatory flow)

Every astrology-touching answer: 1 identify intent → 2 check required data (birth date/time/place, current location) → 3 sufficiency decision (drives the confidence state) → 4 call engine/API services → 5 receive typed facts → 6 validate (schema, ranges, cross-source status) → 7 pass only validated fact-IDs to interpretation → 8 generate from templates → 9 grounding validation (every claim cites a fact-ID; numbers match facts verbatim) → 10 respond with “Why this?” attached (facts, sources, confidence, in-locale). The AI must never: guess chart values; invent transits or muhurats; infer a lagna without adequate birth time; fabricate festival dates; produce lucky numbers outside the numerology engine; state false precision; serve cached data for the wrong timezone/location (cache keys embed location+tz, §7.3); use a family member’s chart without complete profile data (missing data → Tara asks or declines, in-locale).

7.1 Morning-brief generation (the hard batch problem — design)

Users expect the brief at THEIR wake time. Design: per-user brief_time (default 07:00 local) + timezone → Celery Beat ticks every 15 min, enqueuing a generation wave for users whose local brief_time falls 90–30 min ahead (staggered lead). Pipeline per user (idempotency key user+date): fetch chart facts (cached, Layer A) → panchang facts (from panchang_cache, populated once per date+geohash+tradition by a global pre-job at 00:30 local-region time — thousands of users share one panchang doc) → ranking engine picks modules → template composition + LLM polish (batched, low-temperature, prompt-cached) → grounding validation → TTS pre-render (only for users with voice-brief enabled and open-rate >20% trailing — cost control; others synthesize on first open) → store daily_briefings → notification enqueued for exact local time. Priority queues: paying users > trial > dormant (dormant users get on-open generation only — no waste). Retries: 3× exponential; a failed brief degrades to “verified core cards” (panchang + one chart theme, no LLM) rather than nothing; late-arriving data (e.g. user flew to London overnight — location change event) triggers a targeted regenerate. Load smoothing: waves spread across the 60-min lead window hashed by user_id; IST 07:00 is the global spike (≈60% of India users in one band) — the 90-min lead + shared panchang cache + prompt caching keeps the Claude call the only per-user marginal cost. Cost lever: if the morning queue depth breaches SLO, ranking-engine-only briefs (no LLM polish) ship first and upgrade lazily.

9. LLM and Conversation Architecture

Model strategy: primary Claude (latest Sonnet-class for conversations; Haiku-class for classification/ranking polish) via a thin model-abstraction layer (LiteLLM-style adapter, ours) that pins versions, routes by task, and enables a fallback provider (secondary frontier model) behind the same interface — personality prompt tested on both quarterly. Prompt caching everywhere (system prompt + persona + locale style guide are stable prefixes). Retrieval: memory top-k via Atlas Vector Search + recency/type filters (§6.4) with visibility gating; facts via tool calls to astrology/numerology services — the LLM requests facts, never computes them. Structured outputs (JSON schema) for: intent routing, memory-chip extraction, safety labels, avatar-state tags. Low-temperature (0.2) for guidance composition; 0.7 only for small talk. Token budgets: rolling conversation summary (Haiku) keeps context <8K tokens; per-turn hard cap; per-user daily soft cap with graceful in-locale notice. Cost controls: Langfuse per-conversation cost traces, per-language dashboards, alarms at ₹/DAU thresholds; morning-brief LLM polish batchable and skippable (§7.1). Safety & validators in-line: L1 classifier pre-check (self-harm, medical, legal, financial-risk, minors, abuse) → constrained modes L2–L5 (v1 ladder carried forward; astrology framing REMOVED at L2+); grounding validator post-generation (fact-ID citation check, numeric verbatim check); language-quality validator (locale match, script match, register/honorific lint, glossary compliance) — fail → one corrective regeneration → fail → safe fallback line + human review queue; safety post-check on the final text (fear-selling lint: no death/divorce/ruin predictions, no urgency manufacturing). 23. Notification System — Complete Specification (v2.1 addendum)

§10-21, §18 and §22.6 defined the product rules and infrastructure lead times. This section completes the delivery platform so notifications are a designed system, not a side effect.

23.1 Message classes (behaviour differs by class — hard-coded, not configurable per template)

Class T — Transactional (OTP, payment receipts/failures, mandate pre-debit notices, security alerts, data-export ready, L4 safety resources): bypasses quiet hours, bypasses the 3/day cap, never suppressed, highest queue priority, no marketing content ever. Class D — Daily-loop (morning brief, optional night nudge): scheduled to the user’s local clock, subject to quiet hours and per-user time preferences, expires (below). Class C — Contextual (max 1/day): earned, never filler — trigger catalogue in §23.2. Class M — Marketing/lifecycle (founding-offer, referral, win-back): separate legal consent (default OFF), separate WhatsApp marketing templates, hard-capped 2/week, one-tap unsubscribe per channel. Payment-dunning nudges (§22.13) are Class T (utility), not marketing — they concern an existing mandate.

23.2 Contextual-trigger catalogue (the 1/day slot, priority-ordered)

Eligible triggers, highest wins, tie-broken by user’s engagement history: (1) user-requested reminder (“remind me before the 3pm call”) — always wins, and does NOT consume the contextual slot (it is Class T, user-initiated); (2) muhurat window the user asked about is approaching (≤2h); (3) festival today/tomorrow relevant to the user’s tradition + family reminders (mother’s birthday per family profile); (4) follow-up on an open concern from last night’s reflection (“you wanted to revisit the lease decision today”); (5) significant transit change flagged by the ranking engine with relevance score above threshold; (6) streak-free re-engagement: 3+ quiet days → ONE gentle check-in per week maximum, warm not guilt-based (“Tara has your Thursday brief ready when you want it”). Nothing else qualifies. Every contextual send records its trigger ID — the admin dashboard shows the trigger mix, and any trigger with open-rate <15% trailing 14 days is auto-paused and flagged.

23.3 Channel routing & fallback ladder

Per-user channel state machine, evaluated per message: Daily-loop: primary = user’s chosen channel (default: push if PWA installed + permission granted; else WhatsApp if opted in; else email). If push delivery fails or the subscription is expired → silent fallback to WhatsApp (if opted in) same message, NOT both — cross-channel dedupe key user+message_id in Redis, 24h. Transactional: OTP → SMS-grade path (WhatsApp OTP template + email simultaneously if no phone; push never used for OTP); payment/security → all opted-in channels (deliberate redundancy). Marketing: only the explicitly-consented channel(s), no fallback. WhatsApp specifics: Meta-compliant explicit opt-in captured in onboarding step 21 (checkbox + number confirmation, recorded in consents); utility vs marketing template categories mapped to classes D/T vs M (billing differs — utility conversation pricing budgeted at ₹0.35/msg planning rate, marketing ₹0.85); the 24h customer-service window is used for reply-driven flows (user replies to a brief → session messages are free-form until window closes). Email: SES with per-class configuration sets, one-click unsubscribe headers (List-Unsubscribe) on Class M, never on Class T.

23.4 Expiry, collapse & relevance protection

Every message carries expires_at: morning brief push expires at 12:00 local (undelivered → dropped, not late-delivered; the brief itself is in-app regardless); night nudge expires at 23:30 local; contextual triggers carry trigger-specific TTLs (muhurat reminder expires when the window opens); Class T never expires except OTP (10 min). Collapse keys ensure a re-generated brief replaces, never duplicates, its push. Delivery is idempotent on user+message_id end-to-end (queue → provider adapter → provider message-id stored).

23.5 Preference centre (user-facing, in-locale, screen 22)

Per-category toggles (morning / night / contextual / festival / marketing) × per-channel (push / WhatsApp / email) matrix with honest copy — no “are you sure” guilt modals; quiet hours (default 22:30–07:00 local, user-adjustable); brief time picker; one-tap “pause everything for a week” (Class T exempt, stated plainly); travel behaviour toggle: “follow my timezone” (default, uses location events) vs “keep home time”. Changes apply within 60s (preferences cached in Redis with pub/sub invalidation).

23.6 Token & opt-in lifecycle
