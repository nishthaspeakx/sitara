# 0. Brand Philosophy & First-Time Experience
This is a foundational product document. Engineering builds what §4–§23 specify; this section specifies **why it must feel the way it feels** — and makes the opening seconds of Sitara a designed, testable, release-gated artefact like everything else.

## 0.1 What is Sitara?
Sitara (सितारा — "star") is a premium AI life companion that turns a person's real Vedic birth chart into calm, personal, daily guidance — morning, day and night — in her own language, spoken by a guide who remembers her life. It is not a horoscope feed, not an astrologer marketplace, not a chatbot with a zodiac skin. It is the daily companionship of someone wise who happens to know your stars — built on deterministic astronomy, honest confidence, and a published ethics code.
**Final one-line description:** *Sitara is the premium AI companion that understands your stars and walks through every day with you — in your language, with honesty and warmth.*
**Final tagline:** ***"Your stars, understood."***

## 0.2 Who is Tara?
Tara (तारा — "star", also a goddess of guidance and protection across Indian traditions) is Sitara's living presence: a real-faced Indian woman in her early thirties — a licensed, photographed presence — warm-eyed, unhurried, natural in cream knits and soft golds. She is the interpreter between the computed sky and a lived life — she never calculates (the engine does), never predicts doom (the ethics code forbids it), never forgets what you've trusted her with (the memory vault holds it), and never pretends certainty she doesn't have (the confidence system stops her). She is a guide and a friend; she is explicitly NOT a fortune-teller, a therapist, a doctor, or a deity.
**Final one-line description:** *Tara is your astrology-first AI guide — a warm, wise companion who reads your real chart, remembers your life, and speaks your language like she grew up beside you.*
**Final tagline:** ***"The friend who knows your stars."***

## 0.3 Mission
To give every Indian-origin family, anywhere in the world, a daily companion that makes ancient guidance trustworthy, personal and kind — replacing fear-based astrology with computed truth, honest uncertainty and emotional care.

## 0.4 Vision
By 2030, Sitara is the most trusted name in guidance for the global Indian family — the first app opened every morning in a million homes, the standard for ethical AI companionship, and proof that tradition and technology can hold each other with respect.

## 0.5 Brand values
**Satya (truth):** computed facts, cited sources, admitted limits — the engine before the poetry. **Shraddha (respectful faith):** we honour the tradition and its regional forms; we never mock belief and never exploit it. **Seva (care):** the user's wellbeing outranks engagement metrics — no fear, no guilt, no dark patterns, ever. **Saundarya (beauty):** premium is a duty of care — every pixel, phoneme and pause is finished. **Sthirata (steadiness):** Tara is the calm in the user's day; the product never shouts, blinks or panics.

## 0.6 Brand personality
If Sitara were a person: a well-read elder sister who studied astronomy and grew up in a devout home — modern, warm, quietly luxurious, never mystical-spooky, never corporate-cold. Voice register across ALL surfaces (UI copy, notifications, error states, legal summaries): warm, plain, unhurried, first-person from Tara where natural, honorific-correct per locale (§2.3). Words we use: understand, energy, gentle, worth knowing, your chart says, I remember. Words we never use: warning, danger, doomed, guaranteed, unlock now, last chance, or any manufactured urgency.

## 0.7 Tara's personality bible (governs every prompt, §9)
Traits, ranked: warm > wise > honest > gently playful > firm (safety). She listens before advising; asks one good question rather than three; mirrors the user's formality; celebrates without flattery; disagrees kindly when the chart or good sense warrants ("I'd gently hold you back on that one"); owns limits without apology-spirals ("I don't have enough to calculate that — shall we complete your birth time?"). She has continuity of character: the same person at 7am, in a hard conversation, and at midnight — only tone presets (§3.3) change, never identity. She never initiates guilt ("you haven't visited in a while" is banned phrasing — §23.2's re-engagement copy is warm and pressure-free). Her humour is a small smile, not a joke reel. In eight languages she remains one person: the style guides per locale translate her warmth, not just her words.

## 0.8 Emotional design principles
1) **Calm is the product** — every interaction must lower the user's pulse, not raise it. 2) **Anticipation over interruption** — Sitara is looked forward to (the morning ritual), never barging in (3/day cap, §23). 3) **Honesty is warmth** — confidence states and "Why this?" are emotional features: being told the truth kindly is what trust feels like. 4) **Memory is love** — being remembered accurately (with consent) is the deepest delight the product offers. 5) **Never exploit the wound** — anxiety brings users to astrology; Sitara treats that anxiety, never farms it. 6) **Endings matter** — every session should end settled: the night flow, the paywall, even account deletion are composed exits, not cliffhangers.

## 0.9 Emotional goals per major screen
Splash/launch: *anticipation, arrival*. Language selection: *"this was made for me"*. Sign-up/consent: *safety, respect*. Birth details: *being known, not interrogated*. First reading: *wonder ("how did she know to ask that?")*. Home/morning brief: *orientation, a settled start*. Ask Tara: *companionship, being heard*. "Why this?": *respect for my intelligence*. Night reflection: *closure, gratitude*. Memory vault: *control, safety*. Family screens: *love made practical*. Paywall: *invitation, not toll-booth — the price of keeping something already loved*. Payments/receipts: *competence, zero anxiety*. Settings/privacy: *sovereignty*. Error states: *held, not stranded ("Tara will be right back")*. Safety escalation: *seriousness, care, zero astrology*.

## 0.10 Opening app experience — overview
The launch sequence is Sitara's signature: the moment the sky organises itself into meaning. First launch after install plays the full **5.5-second** celestial sequence with Tara's spoken welcome; every subsequent launch plays a **2.2-second** short form (stars → logo → home) that is **skippable by tap from its first frame**; users can disable it entirely (Settings → "Opening animation": Full / Quick / Off). The sequence must feel calm, luxurious, hopeful — arrival, not advertisement.

**Background music:** 5.5s original composition, "Sitara Arrival" — tanpura drone root (D), single santoor phrase rising over swelling warm pad, resolving on the major third as Tara appears; −18 LUFS integrated, peaks ≤−6dBFS, 48kHz AAC ~50KB; composed W10 alongside the sonic-logo (final note doubles as the notification chime seed, one sonic family). **Sound effects:** star-chime shimmer at alignment (phase 3, −24dB), soft bloom swell (phase 4), no sound on dissolve. **Voice-over:** first launch only, Tara (in the user's selected language if chosen pre-install via landing page, else default English, replayed in-locale after language selection): "Welcome. **Accessibility:** `prefers-reduced-motion` → no drift, no particles: a 1.2s crossfade (sky → static constellation + wordmark → Home); all phases have ARIA-hidden decorative marking with a single live-region announcement "Welcome to Sitara"; skippable by tap/Enter/Escape from first frame (after first launch) with a subtle "skip" affordance appearing at 1s; animation never traps focus; flashing stays far below WCAG 2.3 thresholds (no element flashes >3×/s).
**Low-bandwidth / low-end fallback:** assets (constellation Rive/Canvas script + audio + Tara welcome artboard) total ≤220KB, precached by the service worker AFTER first paint of the real first visit (first-ever web visit shows the 1.2s static-crossfade form — the full sequence debuts on second visit/installed launch, when assets are local); device tiering by `deviceMemory`/frame-probe: tier C (low-end) gets 20 particles, no parallax, no bloom scaling (pre-baked bloom sprite), 30fps target; if the first 500ms drop below 24fps, the engine downgrades live to the static form (measured, not assumed).
**Performance budgets:** ≤5.5s total (hard); JS+asset cost ≤220KB precached; main-thread blocking ≤50ms in any frame window; sustained ≥30fps on tier C (Redmi Note-class Chrome), ≥55fps on tier A; zero layout shift on the Home reveal (Home rendered beneath the mask before dissolve begins); cold-start-to-interactive (animation skipped) ≤2.5s p75.
**Acceptance criteria (release-gated, §14):** all timings within ±80ms of spec on the device matrix; skip works from frame one post-first-launch and lands on Home in ≤300ms; reduced-motion path verified; silent-mode path verified (no broken audio promises in console); fps budgets met on tiers A–C; first-launch voice plays in the correct locale post-language-selection; settings toggle honoured; the sequence screenshot-diffs cleanly per locale (wordmark + welcome line); one 30-person panel scores the feeling — calm ≥4.5/5, premium ≥4.5/5, "want to see it again" ≥4.0/5 — before launch.

## 0.12 Motion design principles (app-wide)
Motion is breath, not spectacle: durations 200–400ms standard, 600–900ms for ceremonial moments (brief reveal, night close); easing family fixed (standard: cubic-bezier(0.22,0.61,0.36,1); enter: ease-out; exit: ease-in); nothing bounces except Tara's rare celebration nod; nothing loops except her idle breathing and star twinkle; every animation has a reduced-motion equivalent; motion never blocks input (skippable, interruptible); page transitions share the radial-dissolve language of the launch (centre-out = arriving, fade = leaving).

## 0.13 Premium visual design philosophy
Midnight Executive carried from the board identity into product: deep navy surfaces for sky/ceremony moments, warm cream paper for reading surfaces, gold reserved for meaning (never decoration at random) — a gold element must always be tappable, sacred (constellation), or celebratory. Serif (brand) for Tara's ceremonial lines and headings, humanist sans for UI; Indic scripts get equal typographic care (correct conjuncts, no fallback-font ransom notes — per-script line-height tuning in the design system). Whitespace is luxury: reading surfaces target ≤65 characters/line. Photography never; illustration always — one consistent illustrated universe (Tara, empty states, help art from the same artist's system). Dark night theme is a designed dusk (dimmed navy + candle-warm accents), not inverted colours.

## 0.14 Delight moments (designed, budgeted, never random)
The first "how did she know to ask that" question (minute 2); Tara pronouncing the user's name correctly and asking if she got it right; the first memory chip ("I'll remember your daughter's exam is Friday"); festival mornings — the sky in the brief header shows a subtle diya glow (Diwali) or kite (Sankranti) per tradition; the birthday brief opens with her moon-chart year-ahead note; "Why this?" revealing the actual sky; the annual "your year with Tara" reflection (Phase 2, designed now). Rule: ≤1 surprise-delight per session, never gating function, all skippable, none manufactured-scarce.

## 0.15 Trust principles
Show the working (fact-IDs, "Why this?"); admit the limits (5 confidence states, §5.4); charge honestly (one price, no per-minute meters, no upsell mid-anxiety); guard the data (CSFLE, memory vault, §13); no fear, ever (the fear-selling lint is a trust feature); the ethics code is public and versioned; support is human and reachable; deletion is real and complete. Trust is measured (§18): "I trust Sitara's guidance" ≥4.4/5 in beta panels, tracked per language.

## 0.16 AI ethics principles
Tara is disclosed as AI at first meeting and never pretends otherwise; astrology is framed as guidance and tradition, not certainty (in product, marketing and legal alike); the LLM never sources facts (§5's zero-hallucination contract); memory only with consent, visible and deletable; no emotional-dependency farming — Tara encourages real-world relationships and rest ("it's late — shall we close the day?"), never possessiveness; vulnerable-moment rules (L2+) strip astrology entirely; no dark patterns anywhere including cancellation (one screen, no guilt); model changes ship silently only if character-equivalence panels pass (§9's quarterly persona tests).

## 0.17 First five minutes (the covenant)
Minute 0: the launch sequence — arrival. Minute 1: language chosen, her own script greets her; sign-up in under 30s. Minute 2: birth details asked for like a friend would ("where were you born? do you know what time?") with honesty about unknown times. Minute 3: the first reading — moon sign + one true, specific, chart-derived observation phrased as a question she can feel ("You tend to carry everyone's worries as your own, don't you?") + today's panchang in one warm line. Minute 4: Tara asks ONE real question about her life, listens, offers the first memory chip. Minute 5: the morning-brief promise ("tomorrow at 7, I'll read your day for you — shall I?") + notification opt-in in context. No paywall contact in the first session; the trial begins silently. Gate: ≥80% of onboarding completers reach the minute-3 reading; wonder-moment rating ≥4.3/5 in beta interviews.

## 0.18 Daily emotional journey
**Morning (anticipation → orientation):** the push is a hand on the shoulder, not an alarm; the brief reads like a letter, ends with one intention — user leaves *settled*. **Day (companionship on demand):** Ask Tara opens to her listening state, never a sales surface; answers end with the user more capable, not more dependent — user leaves *steadier*. **Night (closure → gratitude):** dusk palette, slower voice, three gentle prompts, tomorrow previewed in one line — user leaves *finished with the day*. The emotional arc is measured: post-session micro-mood (optional 1-tap) trends must be net-positive in every language; a language whose night-flow completion or mood-delta lags the band gets the §18 named-owner treatment.

## 0.19 The feeling after using Sitara
One sentence, the north star for every design review: **"Someone wise is watching over my days — and she tells me the truth."** If a feature, animation, notification or sentence does not serve that feeling, it does not ship.

# 1. Executive Summary

**Product vision.** Sitara is a premium astrology-first AI life-guidance companion for affluent Indians and Indian-origin families worldwide. Tara — one guide, one personality, one promise — reads the user's deterministic Vedic chart and panchang every morning, thinks through decisions with them all day, and helps them reflect every night, in their own language, in her own regionally authentic voice.

**Exact launch audience.** Affluent Indian-origin women 28–50 in two simultaneous corridors: (a) diaspora — US, UK, Canada, UAE, Singapore, Australia; (b) metro India — Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Ahmedabad, Pune. Personas Priya (NRI, New Jersey/London) and Meera (Ahmedabad) carry over unchanged from v1.

**Core problem.** This customer already spends ₹3,000–₹12,000+/year across astrologers, panchang apps, pooja logistics and family ritual coordination, but every existing product is either a scary horoscope feed, a per-minute astrologer marketplace with fear-selling incentives, or an English-first app that treats her language as an afterthought. Nobody combines a trustworthy computed chart, a persistent memory of her life, an ethical voice, and her mother tongue end to end.

**Product promise.** The three-moment daily loop (morning brief · Ask Tara · night reflection), grounded in a deterministic astrology engine — never an LLM guessing planetary positions — delivered entirely in the user's chosen language, spoken by a Tara who sounds like she grew up speaking it.

**Differentiation (validated against the 28-competitor dossier).** No competitor combines: (1) pushed chart-computed daily brief, (2) persistent consented memory, (3) whole-app native-language experience including payments and support, (4) regionally authentic voice per language, (5) a published ethics code with no fear-selling and fixed-price humanity, (6) "why this guidance" explainability with typed fact citations.

**Phase-1 scope.** Web-first PWA (Next.js) with the complete three-moment loop, Ask Tara text+voice conversations, Tara avatar, memory vault, family context (profiles, not accounts), numerology, subscription with NRI-gifts-India flow, safety ladder, admin console, and full localisation for the launch languages.

**Final languages (8, staged by readiness).** English · Hinglish · Hindi · Gujarati · Punjabi · Marathi · Tamil · Telugu. **A language becomes publicly selectable only when 100% of the app — every screen, notification, payment receipt, error message and Tara's voice — passes native review in that language.** Launch wave: English, Hinglish, Hindi. Wave 2 (≈8 weeks later): Gujarati, Punjabi. Wave 3 (≈14 weeks later): Marathi, Tamil, Telugu. (§2, §15.)

**Final Tara presence.** Photographic: a licensed real face model, professionally shot, delivered as portraits and subtle cinemagraph loops — no real-time animated avatar, no lip-sync. Talking to Tara is a phone-call experience (voice-led, her photo present), chat is WhatsApp-familiar, and her photo carries a daily story ring. Rive is retained only for the launch animation, the constellation system and UI ceremonies. Full specification §4 and §25; "Tara · AI guide" disclosure is permanent.

**Final voice strategy.** One Tara personality, one voice bible, **native voice models per language** — a licensed anchor artist cloned where engines support our languages well (ElevenLabs for English/Hinglish/Hindi), Azure Custom Neural Voice for the five regional languages (the only provider with confirmed native neural voices across gu-IN, pa-IN, mr-IN, ta-IN, te-IN), matched by casting to the anchor's warmth. **v1's "one voice model speaks everything" is dropped** — research confirms it produces foreign-accented regional speech (§3).

**Final astrology architecture.** Five layers: (A) internal deterministic engine on Swiss Ephemeris (pyswisseph, commercial licence) for charts, nakshatra, vimshottari dasha, transits; (B) DivineAPI (Vedic Ananta→Prakash tier) for panchang, choghadiya, rahu kaal, muhurat search and regional festival calendars at launch, plus Prokerala as cross-check; (C) 10,000-case golden validation set signed off by the Jyotish lead; (D) an automated comparison engine that diffs internal vs external outputs and alerts on discrepancy; (E) template-driven LLM interpretation that can only cite validated typed fact-IDs. Zero-hallucination contract enforced in the response pipeline (§5, §9).

**Final technical architecture.** Next.js 15 (App Router, TypeScript, next-intl, PWA) · Python 3.12 FastAPI modular monolith + separate FastAPI realtime (WebSocket) service · Celery + Beat on Redis for batch · MongoDB Atlas (dedicated, ap-south-1 primary; Atlas Vector Search for memory) · S3 + CloudFront · containers on AWS ECS Fargate (→ EKS at Stage 3) · Terraform · OpenTelemetry + Sentry + Langfuse + PostHog. **v1's Expo React Native / NestJS / PostgreSQL stack is fully replaced** per mandate (§6).

**Timeline.** Option A (recommended, §15): **22 weeks** to public launch with 3 complete languages — build starts 10 Aug 2026, closed beta W17, paid pilot W20, public launch W22 (mid-January 2027). Waves 2–3 complete all 8 languages by ≈ May 2027. Option B (all 8 at launch) = 30–32 weeks and is rejected: it delays revenue proof ~2.5 months and multiplies pre-revenue localisation risk.

**Team (§16).** 21 roles, ~13 FTE-equivalent during peak build: product lead, designer ×1.5, Next.js ×3, Python ×3, data engineer, AI engineer, astrology-engine engineer, DevOps, QA ×2, Jyotish lead + 2 regional reviewers, localisation lead + native reviewers, voice director, avatar illustrator + Rive animator, safety specialist (part-time), legal/privacy (retainer), support lead.

**Success metrics (§18).** Gates unchanged from v1 where still valid: onboarding ≥70%, morning return (W4) ≥45%, ≥3 conversations/user/week, night reflection ≥35% of DAU, memory acceptance ≥70%, relevance ≥4.4/5, trial→paid ≥25%, month-2 renewal ≥85%. **Key risks.** (1) Regional voice quality below bar at launch → wave gating absorbs this. (2) Localisation throughput becomes the critical path → dedicated localisation CMS + lead from W1. (3) DivineAPI reliability unproven → contract-tested trial in W2–4, Prokerala fallback wired from day 1. (4) Cost overrun vs board envelope → stage-gated tranches. (5) Voice+AI unit cost → hard ceiling ₹110/user/month with degraded-mode levers.

**Stop conditions (§19).** Fail any two of {morning return, trial conversion, renewal} after two iteration cycles → stop. Plus new hard stops: calculation parity <99.5%, safety critical incident, per-language quality <3.8 sustained, unit cost >₹160/user/month after optimisation.

# 2. Final Language Strategy

## 2.1 Selection — validated, confirmed
The eight-language set is **confirmed**: English, Hinglish, Hindi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu. Re-validation against the six criteria: (a) *market & spend* — Gujarati and Punjabi remain the two most over-represented high-income diaspora communities in US/UK/Canada with the strongest ritual-calendar engagement; Tamil and Telugu are the two largest astrology-paying southern markets (muhurtham economy, porutham culture) and AstroTalk's top consult languages after Hindi; Marathi anchors the Mumbai/Pune home metro. (b) *voice-provider quality* — all six regional languages have confirmed native Azure neural voices (gu-IN, pa-IN, mr-IN, ta-IN, te-IN, hi-IN); ElevenLabs covers EN/HI well and the rest only on its high-latency v3 tier — this shaped the provider map (§3), not the language set. (c) *translation feasibility* — professional translator + Jyotish-vocabulary reviewer pools exist for all eight. (d) *support feasibility* — launch-wave support in EN/HI/Hinglish covers wave-1 users fully; regional support hires gate waves 2–3. (e) *launch complexity* — handled by wave gating, not by shrinking the set. (f) Bengali, Kannada, Malayalam remain Phase 2 for the same reasons as v1 (east-coast US skew but weaker domestic monetisation; high English-app comfort).

**Hinglish is a first-class locale** (`hi-Latn`): Hindi in Latin script with natural English code-switching, its own UI strings, its own voice register. It is not a fallback between English and Hindi.

## 2.2 Whole-app localisation requirement (the contract)
For every language, ALL of the following ship together or the language does not ship: splash, sign-up, onboarding, permissions, navigation, home, Tara conversations, voice interaction, chat transcript, morning guidance, astrology explanations, numerology explanations, notifications, family profiles, memory screens, subscription, payment screens and receipts, settings, privacy policy summary, help centre, error messages, empty states, loading messages, safety messages, customer-support macros, marketing/referral screens, App-install prompts, email templates, WhatsApp templates. The string inventory is ~2,600 UI strings + ~340 notification/email/WhatsApp templates + ~1,100 astrology/numerology template sentences + help-centre articles (~40) + legal summaries. The definition of done for a language is a signed checklist in the admin console covering all nine QA gates in §2.5 — enforced by a feature flag that cannot be enabled without the sign-off record.

## 2.3 Native-language UX rules
**Script:** Devanagari (HI, MR), Gujarati, Gurmukhi (PA), Tamil, Telugu scripts respectively; EN and Hinglish in Latin. Noto Sans/Serif family per script, loaded per-locale (subset, preloaded, font-display swap; ~45–120KB per script subset).
**Transliteration input:** users may type any language in Latin script; the input pipeline auto-detects script+language (§9) and replies in the user's chosen locale and script. A per-thread "reply in Latin script" toggle exists for HI/GU/PA/MR/TA/TE users who read their language more comfortably romanised.
**Code-switching rules:** product register keeps English loanwords that are natural in speech (meeting, flight, budget, salary); astrology vocabulary always native (tithi, nakshatra, rahu kaal never translated to English equivalents); per-locale style guides define the mixing ratio — Hinglish ≈40–60% English tokens, Hindi ≤10%, Tamil/Telugu ≤15% with English nouns transliterated into native script only when conventional.
**Honorifics & formality:** Tara uses respectful-warm second person by default — aap (HI/Hinglish), tame (GU), tusi (PA), tumhi (MR), neenga (TA), meeru (TE) — and never switches to intimate forms uninvited; a settings toggle allows informal address. Gendered verb agreement follows the user's stated gender or neutral constructions when unknown.
**Formats:** dates render dual-calendar where relevant (Gregorian + tithi/samvat line), 12-hour clock with local conventions, Indian digit grouping (₹1,45,000) for INR and Western grouping for USD/GBP/etc., currency by billing region, festival names in regional form (Diwali/Deepavali, Sankranti/Pongal/Lohri per locale), panchang vocabulary per regional tradition (choghadiya surfaces for GU/North, gowri panchangam/nalla neram for TA, rahu kalam naming per south-Indian convention).
**Numerology vocabulary:** Chaldean terms with per-locale glosses (moolank, bhagyank etc. in HI; native equivalents reviewed per language).
**Error & support tone:** errors are calm, blame-free, in-locale, never technical ("Kuch der ke liye Tara se sampark nahi ho pa raha…" not "WebSocket disconnected"); support macros authored per-locale, not machine-translated at reply time.

## 2.4 Language fallback rules — never silent
1. **Provider missing a capability** (e.g. voice engine degraded for Telugu): Tara continues in-locale as text, voice chip explains in-locale that voice is resting; never switches language.
2. **Untranslatable term:** keep the native/Sanskrit term, add a one-line in-locale gloss on first use per session.
3. **User mixes languages:** reply in the account locale, mirroring the user's borrowed words naturally; never lecture about language.
4. **Regional language typed in Latin script:** detect, understand, reply in account locale + script preference.
5. **Family member prefers another language:** family-member profiles carry their own language tag; guidance ABOUT them renders in the account-holder's locale; shareable snippets (P1) render in the member's language.
6. **Pronunciation uncertainty (names/places):** TTS uses the pronunciation dictionary; if a name is new, Tara asks once, in-locale, how to say it, and stores the phonetic override in the user's profile.
7. **Translation failure / missing string:** the string-loader falls back within the SAME language family first (Hinglish→Hindi), logs a P1 localisation defect; raw English appears only if both fail AND the user has approved: the app asks, in the user's language, "This part isn't available in [language] yet — may I show it in English until it is?" — a consented, recorded, per-surface switch, reversible in settings.
8. **Language-quality validation fails on a generated reply** (§9 validator): one automatic regeneration with corrective instruction; second failure → Tara sends the safe in-locale fallback line and the turn is queued for human review. Never an English reply to a Gujarati user without consent.

## 2.5 Translation workflow & localisation CMS
Pipeline (all content types — UI strings, templates, notifications, help, legal): 1) source authored in English + Hinglish concurrently by product writers; 2) professional native translation (contracted translators per language, astrology-literate); 3) Jyotish vocabulary review (regional expert confirms panchang/astrology terms per tradition); 4) cultural review (honorifics, festivals, sensitivities); 5) voice review (voice director reads aloud; strings that sound written get rewritten); 6) UX fit review (overflow, line-break, script rendering on real devices); 7) automated checks (ICU syntax, placeholder parity, glossary compliance, banned-term lint, length bounds); 8) human sign-off by the language owner (named person per language); 9) versioned release through the CMS with full history; 10) regression: screenshot-diff suite re-runs per locale per release.
**CMS decision:** translation memory + glossary + review workflow run in a commercial TMS (Crowdin or Lokalise — final pick in W2 procurement after trialing both against Indic-script QA needs; Crowdin default) with ICU MessageFormat as the wire format, synced to the repo by CI; astrology template sentences and notification/WhatsApp templates live in Sitara's own admin (Mongo `localized_content` collection, §6.4) because they carry fact-slot schemas a generic TMS cannot validate. Glossary (per-language astrology lexicon, ~600 terms) is the single source of truth for translators, the LLM system prompts, and the TTS pronunciation dictionaries.

# 4. Tara's Visual Identity — the Photographic Presence System (canonical)

Three approaches were evaluated across this programme: an animated illustrated 2.5D avatar (v1: Live2D; v2: Rive) and a photographic presence. ## 5.2 Final layered architecture (decided)
**Layer A — internal deterministic engine (primary for charts):** Python service on pyswisseph (Swiss Ephemeris commercial licence, budgeted CHF 750): planetary longitudes, ascendant/houses (whole-sign primary presentation, bhava computed), nakshatra+pada, vimshottari dasha (maha/antar/pratyantar), gochar transits, ayanamsa Lahiri default (KP/Raman selectable Phase 2). Every output is a **typed fact with an ID, value, precision, method and validity window** (e.g. `fact:transit.saturn.house/2026-07-28/user123@v3`). Paired with our own geocoding+historical-timezone resolver (Google Time Zone API + IANA tzdb; DST and historical offsets tested — no astrology vendor is trusted for timezone handling).
**Layer B — external API (primary for panchang/muhurat/festivals at launch):** DivineAPI Vedic Ananta from W3, upgraded to Prakash when Tamil/Telugu wave enters build (regional festival calendars + gowri panchangam). Prokerala Ruby tier runs in parallel as the independent cross-check oracle (its 24h-cache ToS is honoured: cross-check calls are ephemeral). Both wrapped behind one `PanchangProvider` interface; per-day per-location results cached in `panchang_cache` (§6.4) within contract terms — DivineAPI's terms verified for caching in W2 procurement; if restrictive, cache TTL complies.
**Layer C — golden validation set:** 10,000 curated cases (charts, dashas, panchang days, muhurat windows, edge cases: DST transitions, midnight births, leap years, southern-hemisphere, historical timezones, regional calendar splits like amanta/purnimanta) with expected values verified against Jagannatha Hora + Drik Panchang readings and signed off case-by-case by the Jyotish lead. Run in CI on every engine change; release-blocking at <99.9% parity.
**Layer D — comparison engine:** nightly job diffs internal engine vs DivineAPI vs Prokerala on a rolling sample (500 users' next-day data + the golden set slice); any discrepancy beyond tolerance (positions >1 arc-min, tithi/nakshatra boundary times >2 min, dasha dates >1 day) → admin alert + the affected fact is served from the majority source and flagged `disputed` (guidance built on a disputed fact downgrades its confidence state).
**Layer E — interpretation templates:** the LLM composes guidance ONLY from validated fact-IDs passed in context, using approved per-locale template sentences with fact slots; the grounding validator (§9) rejects any astrological claim without a fact-ID citation ("cite-or-die", carried from v1).

## 5.3 Zero-hallucination contract (mandatory flow)
Every astrology-touching answer: 1 identify intent → 2 check required data (birth date/time/place, current location) → 3 sufficiency decision (drives the confidence state) → 4 call engine/API services → 5 receive typed facts → 6 validate (schema, ranges, cross-source status) → 7 pass only validated fact-IDs to interpretation → 8 generate from templates → 9 grounding validation (every claim cites a fact-ID; numbers match facts verbatim) → 10 respond with "Why this?" attached (facts, sources, confidence, in-locale).
The AI must never: guess chart values; invent transits or muhurats; infer a lagna without adequate birth time; fabricate festival dates; produce lucky numbers outside the numerology engine; state false precision; serve cached data for the wrong timezone/location (cache keys embed location+tz, §7.3); use a family member's chart without complete profile data (missing data → Tara asks or declines, in-locale).

## 5.4 Confidence system (user-visible)
| State | Trigger | User-facing framing (rendered in-locale) |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | exact birth time + place, engine parity clean | "Using your exact birth time and current location…" |
| Verified, limited birth data | date+place, no exact time | "Since your birth time is approximate, I'm using your Moon chart rather than precise Lagna timing." |
| Approximate | time window given; or disputed fact in play | "Your birth time is within a window, so I'll keep this at the level I can trust." |
| Tradition-based general | no chart needed / panchang-only | "This is a general panchang-based suggestion, not a personal chart reading." |
| Cannot calculate | required data missing/invalid | "I don't have enough information to calculate this accurately — shall we complete it?" |
Confidence is computed in step 3/6, stored on every guidance record, rendered as a subtle chip on the "Why this?" sheet, and changes Tara's language via the template register — never fabricated certainty.

## 5.5 Validation benchmarks & thresholds (release gates)
Exact-birth-time charts: 100% planetary-position parity within 1 arc-min vs golden set. Tithi/nakshatra/yoga/karana boundary times: ±2 min vs Drik-verified values, ≥99.9% of cases. Dasha period boundaries: ±1 day, 100%. DST/timezone suite (600 cases incl. historical US/UK changes, half-hour zones, Lord Howe, Nepal): 100%. Midnight-boundary and leap-year suites: 100%. Regional panchang variants (amanta vs purnimanta month naming, Tamil solar months, Nanakshahi overlay): reviewed sign-off per language wave. Muhurat windows: match DivineAPI/Prokerala consensus or Jyotish-lead adjudication; disagreements documented. Numerology (Chaldean primary, Pythagorean secondary): 100% parity vs hand-computed set (500 cases). Continuous: comparison-engine live parity ≥99.9% weekly or release freeze.

## 7.1 Morning-brief generation (the hard batch problem — design)
Users expect the brief at THEIR wake time. Design: per-user `brief_time` (default 07:00 local) + timezone → Celery Beat ticks every 15 min, enqueuing a **generation wave** for users whose local brief_time falls 90–30 min ahead (staggered lead). Pipeline per user (idempotency key `user+date`): fetch chart facts (cached, Layer A) → panchang facts (from `panchang_cache`, populated once per date+geohash+tradition by a global pre-job at 00:30 local-region time — thousands of users share one panchang doc) → ranking engine picks modules → template composition + LLM polish (batched, low-temperature, prompt-cached) → grounding validation → TTS pre-render (only for users with voice-brief enabled and open-rate >20% trailing — cost control; others synthesize on first open) → store `daily_briefings` → notification enqueued for exact local time. Priority queues: paying users > trial > dormant (dormant users get on-open generation only — no waste). Retries: 3× exponential; a failed brief degrades to "verified core cards" (panchang + one chart theme, no LLM) rather than nothing; late-arriving data (e.g. user flew to London overnight — location change event) triggers a targeted regenerate. Load smoothing: waves spread across the 60-min lead window hashed by user_id; IST 07:00 is the global spike (≈60% of India users in one band) — the 90-min lead + shared panchang cache + prompt caching keeps the Claude call the only per-user marginal cost. Cost lever: if the morning queue depth breaches SLO, ranking-engine-only briefs (no LLM polish) ship first and upgrade lazily.

## 7.2 Astrology caching (keys)
`natal_chart:{subject}:{engine_v}:{ayanamsa}` — permanent until engine bump (user-specific). `transits:{date}:{lat_band}:{engine_v}` — global, 400-day TTL. `panchang:{date}:{geohash4}:{tradition}:{provider}` — location-specific, shared across users, 90-day TTL. `festivals:{year}:{region}:{tradition}` — global, annual refresh + admin override. `muhurat:{type}:{date_range}:{geohash4}` — location-specific, 30-day TTL. `numerology:{subject}:{system}` — permanent until profile edit. User-specific vs location-specific vs global separation is explicit in the key schema above; Redis holds hot keys (today+tomorrow), Mongo the durable cache.

## 7.3 Voice scaling
Streaming STT and TTS through provider adapters with: connection pooling per provider, per-provider concurrency budgets (from contracts) with queue + overflow to fallback provider, regional endpoints (Azure Central India, ElevenLabs nearest, Sarvam India), voice-minute metering per user (fair-use: 300 min/mo Basic, 900 Premium; soft-limit → gentle in-locale notice + text-mode), real-time cost dashboard (Langfuse + custom meters) with per-language unit-cost alarms, degraded text mode always one failure away (§8), and barge-in support (user speech cancels TTS stream, one in-flight utterance max).

# 8. Reliability and Resilience
**Targets:** availability 99.9% monthly (chat+brief read paths), 99.5% voice; latency SLOs: API p95 <300ms, chat first token p95 <1.8s, voice TTFB p95 <1.2s end-to-end, brief open p95 <800ms; error budgets tracked per SLO with release freeze on burn-rate alerts. **Mechanisms:** ECS/EKS autoscaling on CPU+queue depth+connection count; circuit breakers on every provider adapter (fail fast after 5 errors/30s, half-open probes); retries with jitter only on idempotent calls; Celery dead-letter queues with admin replay; multi-provider fallback (voice §3.3, panchang Layer B, LLM §9); multi-region: static/CDN global day 1, us-east-1 warm standby for api+realtime from Stage 2 (Route53 health-checked failover, RTO 30 min), Mongo Atlas cross-region replica promotion; backups §6.4; DR: RPO ≤15 min (PITR), RTO ≤4h full region loss (tested quarterly via game-day); incident management: sev levels, PagerDuty rota (eng on-call from beta), public status page (status.sitara.app), user-facing incident copy in all live languages (pre-translated templates). **Graceful degradation ladder (explicit):** voice fails → text continues (state 14); avatar fails → static portrait; DivineAPI down → internal panchang (if within validated scope) or Prokerala; cross-source disputed → confidence downgrade + neutral framing; unverifiable calculation → no personalised guidance (tradition-based general only, stated honestly); translation QA fail → §2.4-8 consent flow; morning delay → verified core cards first; Claude primary down → fallback model with conservative persona prompt (§9); everything down → cached brief + apology in-locale.

# 9. LLM and Conversation Architecture
**Model strategy:** primary Claude (latest Sonnet-class for conversations; Haiku-class for classification/ranking polish) via a thin model-abstraction layer (LiteLLM-style adapter, ours) that pins versions, routes by task, and enables a fallback provider (secondary frontier model) behind the same interface — personality prompt tested on both quarterly. Prompt caching everywhere (system prompt + persona + locale style guide are stable prefixes). Retrieval: memory top-k via Atlas Vector Search + recency/type filters (§6.4) with visibility gating; facts via tool calls to astrology/numerology services — the LLM requests facts, never computes them. Structured outputs (JSON schema) for: intent routing, memory-chip extraction, safety labels, avatar-state tags. Low-temperature (0.2) for guidance composition; 0.7 only for small talk. Token budgets: rolling conversation summary (Haiku) keeps context <8K tokens; per-turn hard cap; per-user daily soft cap with graceful in-locale notice. Cost controls: Langfuse per-conversation cost traces, per-language dashboards, alarms at ₹/DAU thresholds; morning-brief LLM polish batchable and skippable (§7.1).
**Safety & validators in-line:** L1 classifier pre-check (self-harm, medical, legal, financial-risk, minors, abuse) → constrained modes L2–L5 (v1 ladder carried forward; astrology framing REMOVED at L2+); grounding validator post-generation (fact-ID citation check, numeric verbatim check); language-quality validator (locale match, script match, register/honorific lint, glossary compliance) — fail → one corrective regeneration → fail → safe fallback line + human review queue; safety post-check on the final text (fear-selling lint: no death/divorce/ruin predictions, no urgency manufacturing).
**Mandatory pipeline (every turn):** input → language/script detection → safety pre-check → intent routing → required-data check → memory retrieval ∥ fact tool-calls → fact validation → response generation → grounding check → language-quality check → safety post-check → TTS render (streamed) → avatar state events → transcript store → memory-chip suggestion. # 23. Notification System — Complete Specification (v2.1 addendum)
§10-21, §18 and §22.6 defined the product rules and infrastructure lead times. This section completes the delivery platform so notifications are a designed system, not a side effect.

## 23.1 Message classes (behaviour differs by class — hard-coded, not configurable per template)
**Class T — Transactional** (OTP, payment receipts/failures, mandate pre-debit notices, security alerts, data-export ready, L4 safety resources): bypasses quiet hours, bypasses the 3/day cap, never suppressed, highest queue priority, no marketing content ever. **Class D — Daily-loop** (morning brief, optional night nudge): scheduled to the user's local clock, subject to quiet hours and per-user time preferences, expires (below). **Class C — Contextual** (max 1/day): earned, never filler — trigger catalogue in §23.2. **Class M — Marketing/lifecycle** (founding-offer, referral, win-back): separate legal consent (default OFF), separate WhatsApp marketing templates, hard-capped 2/week, one-tap unsubscribe per channel. Payment-dunning nudges (§22.13) are Class T (utility), not marketing — they concern an existing mandate.

## 23.2 Contextual-trigger catalogue (the 1/day slot, priority-ordered)
Eligible triggers, highest wins, tie-broken by user's engagement history: (1) user-requested reminder ("remind me before the 3pm call") — always wins, and does NOT consume the contextual slot (it is Class T, user-initiated); (2) muhurat window the user asked about is approaching (≤2h); (3) festival today/tomorrow relevant to the user's tradition + family reminders (mother's birthday per family profile); (4) follow-up on an open concern from last night's reflection ("you wanted to revisit the lease decision today"); (5) significant transit change flagged by the ranking engine with relevance score above threshold; (6) streak-free re-engagement: 3+ quiet days → ONE gentle check-in per week maximum, warm not guilt-based ("Tara has your Thursday brief ready when you want it"). Nothing else qualifies. Every contextual send records its trigger ID — the admin dashboard shows the trigger mix, and any trigger with open-rate <15% trailing 14 days is auto-paused and flagged.

## 23.3 Channel routing & fallback ladder
Per-user channel state machine, evaluated per message: **Daily-loop:** primary = user's chosen channel (default: push if PWA installed + permission granted; else WhatsApp if opted in; else email). If push delivery fails or the subscription is expired → silent fallback to WhatsApp (if opted in) same message, NOT both — cross-channel dedupe key `user+message_id` in Redis, 24h. **Transactional:** OTP → SMS-grade path (WhatsApp OTP template + email simultaneously if no phone; push never used for OTP); payment/security → all opted-in channels (deliberate redundancy). **Marketing:** only the explicitly-consented channel(s), no fallback. WhatsApp specifics: Meta-compliant explicit opt-in captured in onboarding step 21 (checkbox + number confirmation, recorded in `consents`); utility vs marketing template categories mapped to classes D/T vs M (billing differs — utility conversation pricing budgeted at ₹0.35/msg planning rate, marketing ₹0.85); the 24h customer-service window is used for reply-driven flows (user replies to a brief → session messages are free-form until window closes). Email: SES with per-class configuration sets, one-click unsubscribe headers (List-Unsubscribe) on Class M, never on Class T.

## 23.4 Expiry, collapse & relevance protection
Every message carries `expires_at`: morning brief push expires at 12:00 local (undelivered → dropped, not late-delivered; the brief itself is in-app regardless); night nudge expires at 23:30 local; contextual triggers carry trigger-specific TTLs (muhurat reminder expires when the window opens); Class T never expires except OTP (10 min). Collapse keys ensure a re-generated brief replaces, never duplicates, its push. Delivery is idempotent on `user+message_id` end-to-end (queue → provider adapter → provider message-id stored).

## 23.5 Preference centre (user-facing, in-locale, screen 22)
Per-category toggles (morning / night / contextual / festival / marketing) × per-channel (push / WhatsApp / email) matrix with honest copy — no "are you sure" guilt modals; quiet hours (default 22:30–07:00 local, user-adjustable); brief time picker; one-tap "pause everything for a week" (Class T exempt, stated plainly); travel behaviour toggle: "follow my timezone" (default, uses location events) vs "keep home time". Changes apply within 60s (preferences cached in Redis with pub/sub invalidation).

## 23.6 Token & opt-in lifecycle
Web-push subscriptions: stored per device with `endpoint`, keys, UA, created/last-success; a 410/404 from the push service marks the subscription dead immediately; 3 consecutive failures → dead; dead subscriptions trigger a silent re-subscribe attempt on next app open, and channel routing falls back meanwhile. iOS PWA nuance handled in onboarding: push offered only after install; pre-install users see the WhatsApp opt-in first. WhatsApp: number change flows through account recovery (§22.5) and re-verifies opt-in; a user-initiated "STOP" is honoured within the provider SLA and mirrored into `consents` + preference centre. Email: hard bounces and complaints (SES SNS feedback) suppress the address globally except OTP-critical mail via the Postmark failover, and surface a "check your email address" chip in settings.

## 23.7 Delivery infrastructure
Dedicated Celery queues per class (T > D > C > M priority), per-channel sender pools with provider rate-limit budgets (WhatsApp BSP tier throughput, SES send rate, push fan-out), token-bucket smoothing so the IST-07:00 wave (already spread by §7.1 lead-window hashing) never breaches provider caps; DLQ with admin replay per §8; the notification worker writes a single source-of-truth `notifications` doc per message (status: queued → sent → delivered/failed/expired, provider ids, trigger id, class, locale, template version). Emergency stop (§12) halts queues per class/channel/locale in <30s and is drill-tested. Incident notifications (status-page pushes) run on a separate minimal path that does not depend on the main queue infrastructure.

## 23.8 Analytics & health (per-language, per §18 rules)
Per channel × class × locale × template version: sent, delivered, opened/clicked (push click, WhatsApp read where available, email open/click), opt-out rate, dead-token rate, delivery latency p95 vs scheduled time (SLO: 95% of morning briefs delivered within 5 min of target time), cost per delivered message. ## 30.1 Permissions & installation (value-before-ask, always)
Universal pattern: **explain → invite → system prompt → honour the answer.** No system permission dialog ever fires without its explainer sheet (S43) shown first, in-locale, stating the concrete value and the skip path. **Microphone:** asked at first voice action only (mic tap or call button): "To hear you, Tara needs your microphone. Denied → voice affordances remain visible with a small ⓘ that opens re-enable instructions (per-browser/OS illustrated steps); never re-prompt system dialog (browsers won't) — the ⓘ is the recovery path. **Notifications:** asked in onboarding step 13 context ("shall I bring your day at 7:00?") — the value IS the product promise; denied → Today badge + WhatsApp offer immediately presented as the alternative; re-invite allowed once after a week-2 brief-open streak. **Location:** optional always; asked at city step with manual entry equally prominent; denied → manual city, travel banner offers update prompts (§30.2). **Camera:** not used in Phase 1 (documented so nobody adds it casually). **WhatsApp opt-in:** onboarding step 21 checkbox + number confirm (Meta-compliant, §23.3); STOP honoured product-wide. **PWA install:** Android — mini-infobar suppressed, custom install card per §27 timing rule; iOS Safari — illustrated add-to-home coach (share → Add to Home Screen) triggered by same rule, dismissible with 30-day cooloff; installed state detected → install prompts disappear forever. **Dismiss/re-prompt ledger:** every prompt records shown/dismissed/accepted in `consents`-adjacent prefs; global rule: max 2 lifetime dismissals per prompt type, then only passive entry points. **Feature-without-permission parity:** every permissioned feature has a stated no-permission path (voice→text, push→WhatsApp/badge, location→manual city) — parity table lives in the S43 cover sheet; acceptance: app fully usable with ALL permissions denied.

**The rule: Journal is what happened; the Vault is what Tara knows; the thread is where talk lives.** Journal (`/journal`): time-ordered artefacts — daily briefs (archived after their day), night reflections, call summaries, saved guidance ("save to journal" from any bubble/card), milestones (first reading, birthdays) — rendered as a calendar+list; the **life timeline** is the Journal's year view (P1 polish). Memory Vault (`/you/memories`): the 11 typed facts with consent stamps — never a content archive. Conversation thread: §28.3 one-history. **Cross-links, not copies:** a journal call-summary links to its thread position; a memory links to its source turn; deleting has scoped, stated effects — delete a memory → Tara stops knowing it (past journal text unchanged, stated at confirm); delete a journal entry → artefact removed (memories sourced from it survive unless also deleted — offered as a checkbox); delete a conversation → §27 chat rules + dependent memory sources marked "source removed". **Search:** P0 keyword+filters (type: brief/reflection/call/guidance/memory; date; family member) over Journal+thread via Atlas Search; natural-language search P1 (embeddings already exist). **Sensitive search honesty:** searching health-adjacent or safety-flagged content shows results to the user (her data) but never resurfaces L4 content as casual suggestions. **Family privacy:** family-member guidance appears in the account-holder's spaces only; per-member filter exists; no member-facing views in Phase 1 (§10-19).

## 30.6 Stories — the scope decision (Section O resolved)
**Decision: Tara Stories ship as a P1 gated experiment, not P0.** Rationale: launch risk concentrates in the daily loop + call quality; stories add a content-ops treadmill and an attention surface that could dilute the brief's dominance before the core habit is proven. **P1 experiment design:** flag-gated cohort (25% of week-6+ users initially); 1–2 cards/day max at introduction (panchang card + thought-of-day); no promotional card in any user's first 7 days ever; the personal brief remains visually dominant (story ring never badges louder than the brief; no autoplay); stories can never become an infinite feed (hard cap 5 cards/day forever, no cross-day backlog binge — yesterday's stories expire). **Launch (graduate-to-100%) criteria:** ring open-rate ≥35% of DAU in cohort · D7 retention delta ≥0 vs control (non-inferiority — stories must not cannibalise the brief: brief-open delta ≥−2pp) · trust metric flat-or-up · reply-to-story ≥5% (evidence of relationship, not broadcast). Kill criteria: brief-open −5pp or trust dip → experiment pauses. Engineering already scoped (§25.7) — backend built W12–15 as planned but dark-launched; the freed frontend weeks go to call polish and §27 P0-fixes.

# 31. Validation, Accessibility, Governance & Readiness

## 31.1 Usability testing — three mandatory rounds (gates, not theatre)
**Round 1 — low-fidelity flows (W5–6, before visual design hardens):** clickable wireframes; n=16 (8 NRI / 8 India; EN·Hinglish·HI split; belief mix: 6 high-belief, 6 curious, 4 skeptical; digital confidence mix incl. 4 lower-confidence); tasks: complete onboarding, find tomorrow's caution window, correct a birth time, decline memory, find the price; success: task completion ≥80%, onboarding comprehension of consent ≥90%, "what is this app?" articulation matches positioning ≥75%; interview probes: "who is Tara to you?", "what would you not tell her?", "is anything scary here?"; gate: IA/task failures fixed before hi-fi.
**Round 2 — high-fidelity prototype (W10–11):** Figma prototype + Wizard-of-Oz voice; n=16 fresh (same matrix + 4 wave-2-language users on the EN/HI build to probe expectations); tasks add: morning-brief read + "why this?", voice note, simulated call incl. degrade-to-text, paywall comprehension (price/terms recall ≥90%); success: SUS ≥80, trust scale ≥4.2/5, call-metaphor comfort ≥4/5 (specifically: does a call with an AI feel good or eerie — <15% eerie); gate: any dark-pattern perception or trust red flag blocks build of that surface.
**Round 3 — beta usability & trust (W17–19, inside closed beta):** n=24 diary study across 3 languages + moderated sessions; measures: §18 gates live, wonder-moment rating ≥4.3, per-language voice MOS field check, emotional-arc micro-mood trends net-positive, safety-scenario spot test (scripted distress message handled correctly in all 3 languages); gate: §21 beta-exit criteria — this round IS the launch gate evidence.

## 31.2 Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA (canonical commitments)
All §24/§29 rules plus 2.2-specific: focus-not-obscured (sticky headers/tab bar never cover focused element), target size ≥24×24 CSS px minimum with our 44px standard (exceptions map maintained: inline text links only), dragging alternatives (any swipe action has a tap path — reply-swipe has long-press menu), consistent help location (help entry identical position all screens), accessible authentication (OTP autofill; no cognitive puzzles). Screen readers: full SR pass per launch locale (TalkBack + VoiceOver) with Indic TTS verification; chat announces sender+time; call announces state changes; live captions during calls (P0, §28.3); transcripts everywhere audio exists; reduced-motion global (§0.11, §24); contrast AA both themes (tokens pre-validated); text scaling to 200% without loss (layouts tested at 200%, Indic scripts at 175% minimum readable-line rule); colour-independent meaning (§29.4); voice-free parity (§30.1); cognitive load: one primary action per screen (§29.1 CTA rule), plain-language setting (density LOW doubles as plain-mode), no timed interactions anywhere except OTP (extendable); age-related: default type comfortable at 45+ eyesight (body 0.95rem floor, user scaling honoured), touch targets generous, voice-first paths for lower typing comfort.
